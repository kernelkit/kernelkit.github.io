Infix v26.10 is out, with 476 commits in 62 pull requests since v26.09 a
month ago.  Thank you to everyone who sent code, tested release
candidates, and reported bugs.

### Faster status

The big news in this release is speed.  The system now keeps interface,
routing, bridge, Wi-Fi, container and hardware status up to date as
things change, instead of collecting it every time someone asks.
`show interface`, NETCONF and RESTCONF reads, and the WebUI status pages
answer right away.  You notice it most on switches with many ports.

The WebUI makes good use of it.  Status pages refresh on their own, and
the Overview page has new Ports and Health cards that show link state
and LLDP neighbors per port, and anything that needs your attention.

### Quality of Service

Every port now has a QoS configuration: trust PCP or DSCP, map them to
priorities, sort traffic into strict or weighted classes, remark on the
way out, and cap the egress rate.  Ports start with IEEE 802.1Q
defaults, so you only configure what you want to change.  On Marvell
LinkStreet switches it all runs in hardware, so it applies to switched
traffic too.  The [QoS guide][qos] has the details.

### Routing and bridging

- IPv6 dynamic routing, RIPng and OSPFv3, next to RIPv2 and OSPFv2
- A WebUI Routing page that sets up OSPF and RIP in a couple of clicks
- A bridge forwarding database, static entries and `show bridge fdb`
- An MLD querier for IPv6, see [Controlling Multicast on the LAN][mcast]
- Changing an IP address or an STP port cost no longer bounces the link

If you script against NETCONF or RESTCONF, note that the OSPF and RIP
settings moved to new YANG modules, the [release notes][notes] have the
details.

### Getting online

Cellular modems are in: LTE dongles, mPCIe cards and M.2 modules, see
[LTE Modems in Infix][lte].  So is a PPPoE client, for providers that
still require one.  On Wi-Fi, a station and an access point can form a
WDS link for wireless bridges and repeaters, stations roam on their own,
and clients are handed over to a neighbor before their access point goes
down for a reboot or an upgrade.

### New boards

- [Banana Pi BPI-R2 Pro][r2pro], Rockchip RK3568, with Wi-Fi 6E and LTE
- [Banana Pi BPI-M1][m1], Allwinner A20, 32-bit Arm
- [Banana Pi BPI-F3][f3], SpacemiT K1, our second RISC-V board

The Tactical 1000 and other LAN969x boards get a long list of switch,
PTP, and copper PHY fixes, and the BPI-R4 can now [run an MT7615 card
with a blank EEPROM][mt7615].

### Coming up

The x86_64 images now boot from a signed disk image.  This is groundwork
for verified boot on our Arm64 and RISC-V boards, more on that in a
later release.
